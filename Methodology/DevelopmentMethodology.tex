\subsection{Development Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

This project applies the Agile methodology, specifically based on the ScrumBan framework. ScrumBan is a hybrid approach of Scrum and Kanban. Scrum features role assignments, focuses on team coordination, and aims to increment the product's value through predefined iterations (known as sprints). Kanban emphasizes flexibility with continuous workflows, incremental delivery, and strict work-in-progress (WIP) limits \cite{scrum-and-kanban}. 

ScrumBan offers a wide range of options combining Scrum and Kanban methodologies, facilitating its adaptation to the project's context. The main roles defined for this project are a subset of Scrum roles: Product Owner and Development Team (their role descriptions and assignments are found in Section \ref{sec:stakeholders}).

By contrast, the full development cycle reflects the Kanban continuous nature, omitting Scrum's fixed sprints and ceremonies. Since the development team consists of a single developer, traditional Scrum ceremonies such as sprint planning, sprint review, and sprint retrospective are simplified. Instead, regular meetings with the Product Owner are scheduled every 2 days throughout the project to monitor the process.

Before starting the practical implementation, a product backlog (from Scrum) is defined to prioritize the core application features. In addition, a maximum of \textbf{three product backlog tasks} is defined as \textbf{Work In Progress (WIP)}. Following Kanban's pull principles, the number of tasks in progress always reaches the WIP limit, adding next-step tasks if needed to support the continuous workflow.
